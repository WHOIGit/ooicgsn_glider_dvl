Data flow between the notebooks

Notebook 2 builds two datasets from the same downloads: one on the DVL times, which only cover the dives, and one on the glider's engineering times, which cover the dives, climbs, and surfacings. Notebook 4 uses both, and reads the raw bottom track and engineering data again.

1 · Data access and download

Runs on the OOI JupyterHub, where load_kdata works. Each deployment on the FZ line is downloaded in three sections, each logged as ok, partial, missing, or failed.

2 · Process and merge

Corrects the OOINet ADCP data, merges the glider CTD onto the DVL times, and builds the glider dataset with its dives, climbs, and surfacings, linked to the ADCP dives.

4 · Remove the glider motion

Calibrates the flight model on dives and climbs with a pitch offset, then solves each dive for the absolute velocity profile two ways, referenced to the depth-averaged current (Method 1) or to bottom tracking alone (Method 2), and checks each against the other.

Open questions

  • Eastward bias against the depth-averaged current. Method 2's mean eastward difference from the DAC is 2.8 cm/s (0.8 before the pitch offset), though the median magnitude improved to 2.2 cm/s. Possibly a few large differences in the strong currents at the end of the deployment.
  • Stale attitude in the DVL rotation. The DVL rotated its velocities with pitch and heading about 9 s old. Not corrected; it matters during turns and pitch changes.
  • Pitch offset vs depth rate. The −2° offset comes from the horizontal speed; the depth rate alone prefers about +1° and its rms grows by 0.1 cm/s at −2°.
  • Vertical sound-speed correction. Assumes the DVL firmware uses the beam angle for its own sound speed; the other assumption changes Δw by about a quarter (0.1–0.2 mm/s).
  • The upper half of the Method 2 profiles. Above the bottom tracking, no check tests a single dive: the error grows from 3.2 cm/s just above the reference to 6.9 cm/s 40–50 m above it, and most dives extend another 60–70 m above their bottom tracking. The mean errors stay within ±0.6 cm/s, so averages over many dives should hold up better than single profiles.
  • Dives that aren't solved. 373 dives (Method 1) have cells that don't link every depth bin, mostly deep dives with few scatterers.
